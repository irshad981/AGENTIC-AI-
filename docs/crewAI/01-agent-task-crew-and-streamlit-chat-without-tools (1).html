Your First Coverage Officer Agent

A small chat for a made-up insurance company. You paste one car-accident file, ask a question, and read the note. Then you teach the page to offer the three files in a sidebar. One AI worker writes the note. The note does not pay anyone.

How to follow this page. Do Step 1 through Step 18 in that order. Copy every code block exactly as it is shown. From Step 8 on, each app.py block is the whole file: replace app.py with that block, save, and look at the browser before the next step. The code has already been tested for this exercise, so do not rewrite it. After a step, check the sentence that says what you should see. You do not need insurance experience, and you do not need to have used CrewAI before. The first time a special word appears, its meaning is in parentheses beside it.

The story, in plain words

Suraksha General Insurance is a made-up company. It sells car insurance (a contract that says when the company will pay for damage to a car). A customer has an accident and asks the company to pay the garage. That request is a claim (one file: what happened, which contract the customer bought, and the garage bill).

You are the claims officer (the human at the keyboard). You prepare the file. You do not send the money. A senior examiner (a more senior human who is not in this chat) is the only person who can say "pay this" or "do not pay this".

The note you need is a coverage opinion (a first reading of "does this contract seem to cover this accident?"). The opinion is preliminary (a first draft, not the last word) and non-binding (nobody has to pay because of it).

We will build the note-writer as one agent (an AI worker with a job title, a goal, and rules for how it behaves). CrewAI is the toolkit that runs that worker. This agent's job title is coverage officer, so I will call it the coverage officer agent. You will talk to it in a chat (a web page where you type a question and read the reply). First you paste a claim into boxes and read the opinion. Then you add a sidebar (the panel on the left) so the page can offer the three claims. That sidebar page is the one later lessons keep.

You and the agent are two different roles. You ask. The agent answers. On this page the agent cannot look anything up. It has no database (a stored table of records) and no search. Everything it is allowed to know is written into the task (the one job we give it, including the accident story and your question). If a fact is not in that text, the agent must say the fact is missing. It must not guess.

Why one file is harder than the others. Look at claim CLM-20481, which we will use in a moment. A claim id (the short code, such as CLM-20481) is only a label for the file. The policy (the insurance contract) says the company does not pay when the driver has no valid licence (legal permission to drive). An internal note, circular CL-17 (a short office rule that tells staff how to apply the contract), says something softer: if the licence expired within the last 30 days, send the file to a senior examiner. Do not reject it on the spot. In this accident the licence expired 12 days earlier, and the police report does not match what the customer said. A careful reader can hold those sentences together. That reading is the task we will give this agent. We are not asking it to release any money.
These claims are made up. The policy numbers, places, and amounts are for class. Amounts are in Indian rupees (₹, also written INR in the files). The agent's reply is an opinion for a human to read. It must not say the claim has been paid or rejected.

The three claims

You will paste these three files into the page by hand. After those opinions look right, the same files become choices in the sidebar. Read them once now, so the answers later make sense. Own damage means damage to the customer's own car, such as a bumper that needs a repaint. An estimate is the garage's bill for the repair. Papers are the documents in the file, such as the customer's report, photos, or a police report.

How to read this file

role is the job title the agent works under. goal is what "finished" looks like. backstory is the manner I want: quote the policy lines you were given, admit when something is missing, and stop before deciding the money. Words in curly braces, such as {claim_id}, are blanks. They are filled in from the claim when the task starts. llm is the model (the AI that writes the sentences). allow_delegation set to false means this worker cannot hand the job to another worker. # ===== CHANGE THIS FUNCTION =====
def respond(user_message: str, claim: dict) -> str:
    """Answer the question using only the selected claim."""
    crew, defaults = _get_crew()
    inputs = {**defaults, **claim_to_inputs(claim, user_message)}
    result = crew.kickoff(inputs=inputs)
    return result.raw
# ===== END OF THE CHANGE =====

Here is what that function does, in order. load_crew reads crew.jsonc and builds the crew: the coverage officer agent and its task.
